I believe the future is not merely something waiting to be understood; our expectations of the future can also shape reality.

Therefore, I seek to design future-oriented interactive systems that help people reflect on what kind of future technologies we want, and who we hope to become through them.

About

I am currently a Ph.D. student at the College of Design and Innovation (D&I), Tongji University. Advised by Prof. Yang Shi. Previously, I received an M.E. in Mechanical Engineering from the College of Design and Innovation (D&I), Tongji University, under the supervision of Prof. Yunsheng Su, where I was awarded the National Scholarship for Graduate Students. I received a B.E. in Architecture from Zhengzhou University, where I was honored as an Outstanding Student of Henan Province.

My journey from architecture and entrepreneurship to Human–Computer Interaction (HCI) has been driven by two evolving questions: How can we bring design ideas into reality, and what designs should we bring into reality? The first led me to explore design and business, receiving a National Gold Award at the “Internet+” Innovation and Entrepreneurship Competition (one of China's most prestigious university-level competitions), working with several startups, and engaging in seed-stage investment discussions with MiraclePlus. My current research leverages LLM-based multi-agent social simulations to build interactive systems that help people envision possible futures and reflect on the values embedded in their socio-technical designs. Ultimately, I hope to explore through research what is worth building and bring it into the world through practice. I am always happy to meet and learn from people across disciplines who are curious about possible futures and how we might build them together.

Thoughts

Doing research in HCI often leads me down unexpected paths of thinking about people, computing systems, and the possible futures of their interactions. Some of these ideas are half-formed, some are speculative, and few are ready for a paper. Yet I find them worth keeping, as they may inform my future research and design practice in ways I cannot yet anticipate. Not quite sure where they belong, I decided to give them a little home here.
